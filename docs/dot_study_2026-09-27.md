# Dot study: the dot's edge and its faint fade (2026-09-27)

## Purpose

This records the looking-at work done before writing `find_dot`
(`docs/resume_prompt_find_dot.md`).  The code is
`desktop/padanalysis/dot_study.py`.  Every tool in it makes a figure to be
judged by eye; none of them decides anything.

The question behind the later figures: how far out from the dot does its
colour still carry information?  That sets the largest radius `find_dot`
should return.  **No maximum radius has been chosen yet.**  This document
gives the evidence gathered so far; the choice is Bill's.

Why the faint edge matters (Bill): the gas enters the paper over a circle
and may spread sideways through the paper's thickness, so much of the
reaction can lie below the surface and show only as a faint tint around the
edge.  Of the three binding sites, two give yellow and one brown, so the
faint yellows carry information and brown may mean no more than
"saturated".  The rim may carry more than the centre.

## Summary of findings

1. **The dot's edge is found reliably** as the half-way line between the
   pad's white and the dot's inside, and it follows the dot's true shape
   (round in C1032, heart-shaped in C1014).
2. **The fade has two parts.**  Just outside the edge the dot takes out
   10-35 % of the light (depending on the LED); that falls steeply to
   ~2-3 % within 3-5 px, then a slow shoulder of ~1-2 % runs on to ~7-8 px
   and beyond.
3. **The faint edge is worth 17-20 % of the dot.**  Adding up the dot out
   to 7.5 px outside the edge gives 17-20 % more than stopping at the
   half-way line.
4. **Random pixel noise is not what limits the total.**  Out to 12 px the
   noise of the total is 0.55-0.62 "fully dark pixels' worth", against
   totals of 145-507.
5. **The choice of white reference is what limits it.**  The exposed pad
   far from the dot reads 0.04-2.2 % darker than the same pad before
   exposure.  Whether that is a faint real tint or a calibration difference
   is not settled, and at a 7.5 px cutoff it moves the total by 1-5 % --
   6 to 40 times the noise.
6. **The red control mostly shows the same offset.**  photo_red.R, which a
   yellow dot barely absorbs, has its far pad 0.14-1.31 % low across
   C1028-C1036.  So most of the offset is common to every LED.  The blue
   cells usually sit ~0.3-0.5 % above red, which may be a real tint on top.
7. **The darkest dots take out some red** (C1035 about 3.5 %, C1036
   about 5 %, over and above the far-pad offset) -- possibly the brown
   binding site.
8. **The pad edge is close.**  With the pad eroded 4 px, C1032's rings
   reach the pad edge 5.8-7.5 px outside the dot's edge, and C1014's heart
   nearly touches it.  Curves rise again beyond ~15 px, where the rings
   reach the pad's corners.
9. **Exposure does not visibly add pixel noise.**  The far-white pixel
   spread is 0.83-1.0 of the pre-image's.  (The pre-image is shot shorter,
   e.g. 1000 µs against 2193 µs for royal_blue.B, which may make its own
   spread a little larger.)

## Terms used here

- **Layer px** -- one pixel of one Bayer layer (a 2 × 2 block of the
  sensor).  All distances are in layer px.  The bound crop is 130 rows ×
  183 columns; x is the column, y the row, origin top-left.
- **R** -- signal (minus the dark level) divided by the gray-chip divisor.
  It does not depend on the exposure time, so pre-image and measurement
  compare directly.
- **R_white** -- the median R of the pre-image's own dry pad (commit
  eb4d8d2).  **R_inf** = R / R_white, so the dry pad is 1.
- **Far white** -- the median of the *measurement's* pad more than
  `D_FAR` = 8 px outside the dot's edge.
- **Edge, half-way line** -- see `dot_edge` below.  "d px outside the edge"
  means the band d px out from that line, following its shape.
- **Noise floor of a band mean** -- σ / √n, with σ the pixel-to-pixel
  spread (robust: 1.4826 × median absolute deviation) and n the pixels in
  the band.  It assumes neighbouring pixels are independent.
- **Eroded pad** -- the fitted pad rectangle shrunk by `ERODE_WHITE` = 4 px
  so the pad's own edge is left out.  All the work is done inside it.

### How `dot_edge` finds the edge

1. Smooth the image 3 × 3 (NaN skipped) so noise does not fray the line.
2. First guess: the level half-way between the pad's median (mostly white)
   and its 1st percentile (deep in the dot).  Pixels darker than that, the
   largest connected piece with holes filled, are the dot.
3. Refine once: white = median beyond 8 px outside that dot; dot level =
   median more than 2 px inside it.  The half-way level between those two
   gives the final dot.  Holes are filled, so a pale centre still counts as
   inside.
4. Each pixel's signed distance from the edge (positive outside, negative
   inside) comes from `scipy.ndimage.distance_transform_edt`.

### Graphic 2 -- `show_cutoff`, explained step by step

![graphic 2](../desktop/padanalysis/fig/dot_study_C1032_cutoff_cyan.B_R_inf.png)

Figures: `dot_study_C1032_cutoff_violet.B_R_inf.png`,
`dot_study_C1032_cutoff_cyan.B_R_inf.png`,
`dot_study_C1014_cutoff_royal_blue.B_R_inf.png`.

**The question it asks.**  Suppose `find_dot` draws its boundary c px
outside the half-way line and we add up all the dot inside it.  How much do
we get, and how well do we know that total?  Graphic 1 looked at one band
at a time; graphic 2 looks at everything inside the boundary at once.

**The x-axis, for all three charts,** is that cutoff c.  c = 0 is the
half-way line.  c = 4 is a boundary 4 px outside it.  Negative c means a
boundary *inside* the dot (only the dot's deeper part is counted), so the
left end is the dot's centre.

**The unit, "fully dark pixels' worth".**  Each pixel adds
(far white − its R_inf).  A pixel at R_inf 0 on a white of 1 adds 1; a
pixel at 0.99 on a white of 0.997 adds 0.007.  So a total of 250 means as
much light taken out as 250 completely black pixels would take out.
Worked example, C1032 violet.B: the 309 pixels inside the half-way line add
209.6; out to c = 7.5 there are 955 pixels, adding 249.4.  The 646 pixels
of the fade add 40 more, 19 % of the dot.

**Top chart -- the total inside the cutoff.**
- Dark blue: the total measured against far white.  It climbs steeply
  across the dot, bends over at the edge (c = 0), and goes flat by ~7 px.
  Flat means the pixels being added bring no more dot.
- Light blue: the same measured against the dry pad (R_inf 1).  It keeps
  climbing slowly all the way out, because every added pixel brings the
  far-pad offset (0.3-1.7 %) with it.  The gap between the two blue curves
  is the white-reference question in pixels' worth.
- Dotted, right-hand axis: how many pixels are inside the cutoff.
- Gray shading: the boundary has run past the eroded pad, so part of that
  ring is missing and the total under-counts from there on.

**Middle chart -- signal to noise of the total.**  The total divided by
its random noise, σ × √N (N pixels inside).  It peaks at c ≈ −0.5 in all
three cells, at about 460 (cyan.B) to 1050 (C1014).  Read it this way: if the aim were only to *see*
the dot -- to decide there is one -- the best boundary would be the
half-way line, and the dot is so plain (signal/noise in the hundreds) that
detection is no problem.  The peak says nothing about the best boundary for
*measuring how much* dot there is, because it would throw the whole fade
away.  That is what the bottom chart is for.

**Bottom chart, log -- what limits how well the total is known.**  Four
curves, all in the same unit as the top chart.  At any cutoff, the largest
of them is what limits the total there.
- Orange -- **random noise** of the total, σ_pre × √N.  0.3-0.6 over the
  whole range.  Always the smallest.
- Purple -- **uncertainty of the far-white level itself.**  The white is
  one number taken from every pixel, so its error counts N times:
  N × σ_far / √(n_far).  0.2-0.8 for C1032, up to 1.7 for C1014 (only 198
  far-white pixels).
- Blue -- **dot left outside the cutoff**: the total at 8 px minus the
  total at c.  It is large inside the dot, 40 at the half-way line for
  violet.B, 12 at 2 px, 4 at 4 px, 1.2 at 6 px.  It drops to zero at 8 px
  **by construction, not because the dot ends there**: it is measured
  against far white, which starts at 8 px.  Any dot beyond 8 px is not
  counted by it.
- Dotted gray -- **the reference choice**: the dry-pad total minus the
  far-white total.  1-11 at the edge, 3-22 by 7.5 px, still growing.

**What the bottom chart says.**  Close to the edge the blue curve (dot
left outside) is the largest, so moving the boundary out gains more than it
costs.  Somewhere between 3 and 5 px out the dotted gray curve (reference
choice) overtakes it -- violet.B at ~5 px, cyan.B and C1014 at ~3-4 px --
and stays the largest.  Noise (orange) never matters.  So past ~3-5 px the
white-reference question matters more than where the boundary is.  (The
dot left outside keeps shrinking to ~6-7 px; it just stops being the
largest error.)

**No "best cutoff" line is drawn.**  A first version marked where the
combined error was least; it fell at 7.5 px in every cell, only because the
blue curve is forced to zero at 8 px.  It was removed.

- **Cells:** all eight that have a pre-image, all narrow-band LEDs:

  layers sit half a layer px apart on the sensor, small against 1 px.)
- **Bottom x-axis:** ring distance from the royal_blue.B edge.  **Top
  x-axis:** roughly the radius from the dot's centre (distance + r_eq).
- **Y-axis, log:** 1 − (ring mean of exposed R ÷ ring mean of unexposed R),
  the fraction of light the dot takes out in that ring.  The plain ratio is
  not plotted on a log scale because every faint value would sit squashed
  just under 1.  Open markers are negative.  Dotted lines, in each cell's
  colour, are twice that cell's noise floor for a ring mean.
- **Gray shading:** rings clipped by the eroded pad.  For ring *means* this
  only makes the ring partial; it does not bias them the way it biased the
  sums in graphic 2.

**The two choices of R unexposed**, one per chart:

- **Top: the pre-image, pixel for pixel** (recommended).  Its R is shifted
  onto the measurement's grid and averaged over the same pixels as the
  exposed ring.  The paper's own pattern cancels, and nothing is assumed
  about where the dot ends.  Each ring averages 50-200 pixels, which does
  the smoothing, so no extra smoothing is used.  Its risk: any pre/post
  calibration difference shows up as a flat offset.
- **Bottom: the exposed pad's median beyond 8 px.**  Same image and
  exposure, so calibration cancels.  But any tint beyond 8 px is read as
  white, so the tail is forced to zero there.
- Not plotted: the pre-image's dry-pad median (today's R_inf).  Its values
  fall within 0.001-0.004 of the top chart's.

**Findings.**
- Inside the dot every cell is flat, with a slightly paler centre in cyan
  and green (C1032: ~0.50 against 0.55 at the rim).
- Gb and Gr agree closely in both cyan and green.
- In the top chart the blue and green cells hold a faint 0.5-1.5 % out to
  the pad edge, 2-4 × their noise floor.
- The fade's colour changes with distance.  In C1032, relative to violet,
  royal_blue / cyan.B / green go from 0.65 / 0.29 / 0.15 at the edge to
  1.2 / 0.87 / 0.67 at 6.5 px.  In the bottom chart this mostly goes away,
  so it comes largely from the same flat offset added to each cell.
- Beyond ~15 px all curves rise again in both charts: the rings reach the
  pad's corners.  Probably the pad edge, not the dot.

## Open questions -- Bill's to decide

1. **Maximum radius of interest.**  Not decided.  The evidence: against far
   white the fade meets 2× the noise floor at 7.25-7.75 px outside the
   edge; against the dry pad at 9.75-12.75 px; the ring means stay above
   the floor out to the pad edge with the pre-image reference.
2. **Which white?**  Far white (exposed pad beyond 8 px) or the pre-image's
   dry pad.  Most of the 0.04-2.2 % difference is shared by every LED,
   red included, which points to calibration or a non-yellow change of the
   whole pad; the blue cells' extra ~0.3-0.5 % over red may be real tint.
3. **Which noise?**  The pre-image σ or the far-white σ.  They differ by
   less than 20 %.  No zero-concentration exposure exists yet to settle
   whether exposure itself adds noise.
4. **The pad edge.**  Future dots will reach it; C1014 already nearly does.
   The rings must stop at the pad, and a dot that crosses the eroded pad
   needs a different white reference.

## Checks proposed, not done

- **Bare paper below the pad, per cell**: exposed R ÷ pre-image R on paper
  the gas never reached.  Darker there too → calibration, use far white;
  unchanged → the pad really changed, use the dry pad.
- **A zero-concentration exposure**: the same handling with no arsenic,
  to measure both the white offset and any added noise directly.

## Method caveats

- Noise floors assume neighbouring pixels are independent.
- The pre-image is shifted onto the measurement's grid by resampling, which
  smooths its pixel noise; for that reason σ_pre is measured on the
  pre-image's own grid, not the shifted one.
- `D_FAR` = 8 px is a choice; graphic 1's cut barely depends on it, but the
  far-white level does.
- Everything is worked inside the pad eroded by 4 px, which is safe only
  while the dot stays clear of the pad edge (C1028-C1036).
- The step-1, step-2 and graphic-1 edges use the cell's own half-way line;
  graphic 2b uses royal_blue.B's line for every cell.
