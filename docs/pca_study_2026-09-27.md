# PCA study: how to define "yellow" and "brown" (2026-09-27)

## Purpose

`analysis_1.cell_PCA` splits every pad pixel into an amount of yellow and an
amount of brown.  To do that it needs two **reference colors**, one for pure
yellow and one for pure brown.  Today it picks them separately on each pad:
the two most extreme hues found on that pad.  The question for this study:
should the two reference colors be fixed once, from chosen pixels pooled
over many pads, and then used unchanged on every pad?

Code: `desktop/padanalysis/pca_study.py`.  Figures:
`desktop/padanalysis/fig/pca_study_<what>.png`.

## The data

- 18 samples in two sets:
  - **set 1**, C1017–C1025: larger dots with softer edges;
  - **set 2**, C1028–C1036: smaller, sharper dots.
- Concentration is `samples.manual_read` (solution ppb).  Bill corrected
  two of these on 2026-09-27: **C1022 is 2.23 ppb** (was entered as 40) and
  **C1023 is 39.99 ppb** (was 160).  Everything below uses the corrected
  values.
- Every value is absorbance against the **strip's own dry pad in the
  pre-image**, which is the new white reference.  The bare paper below the pad
  is not used anywhere.  (`duotone.PadCube` was changed on 2026-09-27 to do
  this.  Figures made before that change used the bare paper and should be
  ignored.)
- Only channels that have that reference can be used.  All 18 samples share
  **six**: violet.B, royal_blue.B, cyan.B, green.Gb, green.Gr and photo_red.R.
  The two greens are the same LED seen by two sensor layers, so this is five
  wavelengths.

## Terms used here

- **Channel** — one LED seen by one sensor color layer, e.g. `violet.B`.
- **Absorbance** — how much light the pad takes out compared with the dry
  pad, in one channel.  0 = no change; larger = darker.
- **Overall darkness** — the part of a pixel's absorbance that is the same in
  all six channels.  A shadow does exactly this, so it is removed before
  anything else is done (`duotone.contrast`).  What is left says only how the
  six channels *differ* from one another, which is the pixel's hue.
- **The shared map** — a flat picture on which every pixel of every pad is
  placed by its hue.  See the next section.
- **Hue angle** — the direction of a point from the map's origin, in degrees.
- **Strength** — the distance of a point from the map's origin.
- **Ring** — the pixels lying a given distance (1 px band) from the dot's
  edge.  Negative distance = inside the dot.

## How to read the shared map

After overall darkness is removed, each pixel is still a list of six
numbers.  Almost all of the variation among them (98.4 % over the 18 pads)
lies in just two directions, so the pixels can be drawn on a flat map with
those two directions as its axes, without losing much.

**What the two axes mean**, from how much each channel counts toward them:

| channel | axis 1 | axis 2 |
|---|---|---|
| violet.B | +0.52 | −0.51 |
| royal_blue.B | +0.41 | −0.09 |
| cyan.B | +0.09 | +0.37 |
| green.Gb | −0.16 | +0.38 |
| green.Gr | −0.15 | +0.40 |
| photo_red.R | −0.71 | −0.55 |

- **Axis 1 (to the right):** the pixel takes out more violet and royal-blue
  light than red and green light.  Every colored pixel moves to the right.
  Axis 1 holds 89 % of the variation.
- **Axis 2 (up):** the pixel takes out more green and cyan light than violet
  and red light.  Brown does this; yellow takes out mostly violet and so goes
  *down*.  Axis 2 holds 9 % of the variation.

**Angles and distances, in plain terms.**

- The **origin** is white: no change from the dry pad.
- A pixel holding **one dye**, at any amount, lies on a **straight line out
  from the origin**.  More dye = farther out along the same line.  So the
  **direction** of a point is its hue and its **distance** is its strength.
- The **angle** is measured like a clock hand pointing right: 0° points
  straight along axis 1, positive angles point upward (toward brown), and
  negative angles point downward (toward yellow).  The dashed gray spokes on
  every map are drawn at −60°, −30°, +30° and +60° as a guide.
- Both axes are drawn to the **same scale**, so an angle on the page is the
  angle in the tables.
- A pixel holding **a mixture** of two dyes lies **between** the two dyes'
  lines.  If the mixture changes from pixel to pixel, the points no longer
  lie on one straight line.  They follow a **curve** that swings from one
  line toward the other.  That is what "the path bends" means below.

## Step 1 — each pad's own "yellow" and "brown" (`show_own_endmembers`)

Figure: `fig/pca_study_own_endmembers.png`.  One panel per pad, for six pads
spanning 2.2 to 160 ppb.

What is drawn on each panel:
- **light gray dots:** every pixel of all 18 pads, the same on every panel;
- **black dots:** this pad's pixels;
- **orange line:** the "yellow" that the per-pad method picks from this pad
  alone;
- **brown line:** the "brown" it picks;
- **dashed spokes:** the reference directions, labeled in degrees;
- **open circle at the origin:** white (the dry pad).

**Judge:** do the orange and brown lines point the same way on every pad,
and do they run along the pad's own black dots?

**What it shows:**

| pad | ppb | own yellow | own brown | apart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1034 | 2.2 | +152° | +149° | 3° |
| C1032 | 5 | +159° | +179° | 19° |
| C1021 | 20 | −36° | +24° | 60° |
| C1035 | 40 | +179° | −163° | 18° |
| C1024 | 80 | −39° | +28° | 67° |
| C1025 | 160 | −7° | +28° | 35° |

1. **On three of the six pads the per-pad method fails outright.**  On C1034,
   C1032 and C1035 both lines point *backward*, into the cloud of white
   pixels around the origin.  Most of each pad is white.  The method keeps
   "the more strongly colored half" of the pixels, but on these pads that
   cutoff is only about three times the noise, so the half it keeps is
   mostly noise pointing every which way, and the extremes it picks are
   noise.
2. **Where it works, the pads roughly agree:** yellow at about −37° and
   brown at about +26° (C1021, C1024).
3. **On the darkest pad it misses yellow:** C1025 has almost no pure yellow
   pixels, so its "yellow" comes out at −7°, a mixed hue.
4. **Consequence:** the per-pad amounts cannot be compared from pad to pad,
   and on pale pads they are meaningless.  Fixed reference colors are needed.

**The shape of the gray cloud** (all pixels, all pads): it starts at the
origin, runs out in a **straight line at about −30°** (C1034's dot lies
exactly on it: pure pale yellow getting stronger), and then **bends upward**
to end near **+20° to +28°** (the centers of the dark dots).

**Judge:** does the red line sit on the dot's edge, and is there white pad
between the dot and the dotted line?

**What it shows:**
- The red line follows the dot's edge on all 18 pads.
- On **C1021, C1023 and C1025** the dot runs past the dotted line on the
  left, so the red line follows the dotted line there.  C1018 and C1024 just
  touch it.  Their rings are complete inside the dot but **partial** outside
  it on that side.  This matters little for step 2b, which averages each ring.
- **C1022 (2.2 ppb), C1034 (2.2 ppb) and C1020 (5 ppb) have the palest
  dots** (dot level in R_inf 0.38, 0.49 and 0.42, against 0.16–0.30 for every

Dot sizes (radius of a circle of the same area, layer px): set 1, 10.3–19.2;
set 2, 8.7–11.6.

## Step 2b — the color in rings around the dot (`show_ring_paths`)

Figure: `fig/pca_study_ring_paths.png`.

Each pad's pixels are grouped into 1 px rings by their distance from the
dot's edge, from the dot's center out to the pad's white.  Each ring is
averaged, which removes nearly all pixel noise, and the average is placed on
the shared map.  Joining a pad's rings in order gives one **path** per pad.

What is drawn:
- **Left, the map:** one path per pad, from the dot's center (big filled
  marker, labeled with the sample number) out to the origin (white).  The
  **open black circle** on each path is the ring at the dot's edge.
- **Right top:** each ring's hue angle against its distance from the dot's
  edge (negative = inside the dot).  Rings weaker than 0.02 are left out,
  because a nearly white ring has no meaningful hue.
- **Right bottom:** each ring's strength against the same distance.
- **Color of a path:** concentration, darker blue = higher (log scale).  The
  blue is only a code for concentration; it is not the dye's color.
- **Marker:** circle = set 1, square = set 2.

**Judge:** do all paths follow one common curve?  Do pads of the same
concentration end in the same place?  Where does each path turn?

**Numbers behind it** (angle in degrees, strength as on the map):

| pad | ppb | center angle | center strength | edge angle | edge strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1022 | 2.2 | −25 | 0.51 | −30 | 0.31 |
| C1034 | 2.2 | −29 | 0.44 | −31 | 0.28 |
| C1033 | 4.2 | −19 | 0.54 | −23 | 0.34 |
| C1020 | 5 | −26 | 0.50 | −32 | 0.29 |
| C1031 | 5 | −14 | 0.53 | −23 | 0.35 |
| C1032 | 5 | −13 | 0.52 | −19 | 0.35 |
| C1017 | 10 | +1 | 0.55 | −26 | 0.35 |
| C1018 | 10 | +1 | 0.57 | −27 | 0.36 |
| C1019 | 10 | +2 | 0.52 | −28 | 0.36 |
| C1021 | 20 | +22 | 0.51 | −19 | 0.37 |
| C1028 | 20 | +7 | 0.55 | −11 | 0.35 |
| C1029 | 20 | +15 | 0.53 | −12 | 0.34 |
| C1030 | 20 | +2 | 0.59 | −8 | 0.34 |
| C1023 | 40 | +25 | 0.45 | −15 | 0.39 |
| C1035 | 40 | +18 | 0.57 | −8 | 0.36 |
| C1036 | 40 | +22 | 0.55 | −14 | 0.37 |
| C1024 | 80 | +25 | 0.51 | −21 | 0.39 |
| C1025 | 160 | +22 | 0.49 | −3 | 0.40 |

**What it shows:**

1. **Every pad's faint outer fade is yellow.**  Outside the dot's edge the
   rings, at every concentration, lie mostly at −20° to −40° (−12° to −42°
   in all).  All 18 paths leave
   white along the same line at about −30°.  So the pale-yellow direction is
   the same on every pad, and it is well defined.
2. **Inside the dot the hue is steady.**  From about 3 px inside the edge to
   the center, each pad's hue stays nearly constant (right top).  The change
   from the yellow fade to the dot's own hue happens in a narrow band, from
   about 3 px inside the edge to the edge itself.
3. **The dot's hue rises with concentration, then stops rising.**  The center
   angle goes from −25° and −29° at 2.2 ppb, to −13° to −26° at 5 ppb, to
   about +1° at 10 ppb, to +2° to +22° at 20 ppb, to +18° to +25° at 40 ppb,
   +25° at 80 ppb and +22° at 160 ppb.  **From 40 ppb up, all dots end at
   nearly the same hue, +18° to +25°.**  No ring anywhere goes above about
   +26°.
4. **The strength does not follow concentration at all.**  Inside the dot,
   strength is 0.44 to 0.59 on every pad from 2.2 to 160 ppb (right bottom),
   with no trend: the two 2.2 ppb pads read 0.44 and 0.51, and the 160 ppb
   pad reads 0.49.  In this measure, concentration shows up as a change of
   **hue**, not of strength.  (Part of the reason: brown takes out
   light fairly evenly across the channels, and that even part is removed
   along with the shading.  So much of brown's darkness does not appear on
   the map at all.  For C1025's brown pixels, absorbance is 0.63–0.78 in five
   of the six channels.)
5. **How well pads of the same concentration agree:**
   - 2.2 ppb (C1022 set 1, C1034 set 2): −25° and −29°, close;
   - 5 ppb: set 1's C1020 (−26°) is yellower than set 2's C1031 and C1032
     (−14°, −13°);
   - 10 ppb (all set 1): +1° to +2°, close;
   - **20 ppb disagrees most:** set 1's C1021 at +22°, set 2's C1028–C1030
     at +7°, +15° and +2°;
   - 40 ppb (C1023 set 1, C1035 and C1036 set 2): +25°, +18°, +22°, close.
6. **The picture is consistent with two dyes.**  Every ring lies between the
   yellow line (about −30°) and the dark-dot hue (about +25°), and each path
   swings from one toward the other.  One more piece of evidence: dots of
   the same strength sit at different hues (C1020 at 0.50 and −26°; C1032 at
   0.52 and −13°).  So the bend is not simply what any single dye does as it
   gets darker.  It looks like a real change in the mix.

**What this means for fixing the reference colors** (proposals only, not
decided):
- **Yellow** can be taken from the outer fade rings of all pads, or from the
  centers of the palest dots (C1022 and C1034, both 2.2 ppb).  Both lie on
  the same line, about −25° to −30°.
- **Brown** can only be taken from the centers of the darkest dots (40 ppb
  and above: C1023, C1024, C1025, C1035, C1036), which sit at +18° to +25°.
  Whether that is pure brown, or the most brown that these pads ever reach,
  cannot be told from these data.
  Either way it is a usable fixed reference, as long as every pad is
  measured against the same one.

## Plan: from two reference colors to arsenic per pad (proposed 2026-09-27)

Bill's proposal, organized, with an assessment of each step.  Nothing here
is built yet.

### What is known about the chemistry

- **No published reflectance or absorbance spectra of the stain were
  found.**  Every source describes it by color names only:
  "lemon-yellow to brown-black" [2, 3] and "yellow to brown" [4].
- Rahman et al. [1] (the paper `tex/gutzeit_analysis.tex` cites) assign the
  two-site product AsH(HgBr)₂ as yellow and the three-site product
  As(HgBr)₃ as brownish-yellow, which agrees with Bill's recollection.
  - They measured the spot with a color meter and used one overall
    color-difference number (ΔE* in L\*a\*b\*), not a spectrum.
  - ΔE* rose in a straight line up to 30 µg/L, then curved from 30 to
    80 µg/L.
- **A puzzle to keep in mind.** The brown form, As(HgBr)₃, carries three
  mercury per arsenic; the one-site yellow form, AsH₂(HgBr), carries one.
  By simple bookkeeping, then, brown needs the *most* mercury per arsenic,
  yet it appears where arsenic is heaviest.  The published color assignments
  do not explain this.  So: trust the colors we measure, and do not build
  the arithmetic on the one/two/three-site story.
- `tex/gutzeit_analysis.tex` already describes the products as one
  absorption edge that shifts toward red as substitution advances, not as
  separate peaks.  Step 2b agrees: one yellow line, then a gradual swing
  toward brown.
- **So our own images are the best spectral data available.**

### The plan, in order

1. **Fix two reference colors.**  Yellow from the pale dots and the outer
   fades; brown from the centers of the dots at 40 ppb and above.  Both stay
   fixed for every pad.
2. **Choose the channels** (LED and Bayer layer) that measure those two
   colors best.
3. **Build a fixed conversion** that turns each pixel's channel readings
   into a yellow amount and a brown amount.  Computed once, used on every
   new image.
4. **Per-pixel model:** each pixel is white plus some yellow plus some
   brown, and the two colors add without hiding each other.
5. **Calibrate:** fit an arsenic amount from the two color amounts, against
   the known concentrations.
6. **Total per pad:** add up the arsenic over the colored area.
7. **Dots cut off by the pad edge:** fill in the missing parts of each ring
   around the dot, then add them in.

### Assessment, step by step

**1. Reference colors: sound.**
- Yellow is well defined: all 18 pads' outer fades lie on the same line, at
  about −30° on the map (step 2b).
- Brown is only "the most brown these pads reach," and may itself be a mix.
  That is acceptable as long as it stays fixed.
- Plotting both as absorbance against wavelength gives our own spectra of
  the Gutzeit products — the best available answer to the spectral
  question.

**2. Choosing channels: use a different tool than PCA.**
- PCA finds the directions where pixels vary most.  It does not find which
  channels measure two *known* colors most precisely against the noise.
- Once the two colors are fixed, the right tool ranks each channel subset by
  how precisely it measures yellow and brown.  `duotone.j_amounts` and
  `duotone.subset_sigma` already do this; `report14.py` used them on the old
  (bare-paper) data.
- **The limit is which channels have a white reference.**  Only 6 channels
  are shared by all 18 samples.  Amber, red-orange, red and blue cannot be
  ranked unless the pre-image lights them, or they get another white
  reference.

**3. Fixed conversion: correct.**  With fixed colors, the conversion from
channel readings to yellow and brown amounts is one fixed matrix per chosen
channel set.  The only extra step is that negative amounts are set to zero.

**4. Per-pixel model: two changes needed.**
- **Stop removing overall darkness inside the dot.**  That removal was meant
  for the pad-rim shadow.  It also throws away most of brown's darkness,
  because brown absorbs fairly evenly across the channels (0.63–0.78 in five
  of six channels on C1025).  With the dry-pad white and the eroded pad,
  little shading is left to remove.
- **Test K/S as well as absorbance.**  On paper, which scatters light, the
  amounts are more nearly additive in the Kubelka-Munk measure (K/S) than
  in absorbance; `tex/gutzeit_analysis.tex` says the same.  Both are easy
  to try.

**5. Calibration: the biggest risk.**
- **Individual pixels saturate.**  Inside the dot, royal_blue.B R_inf sits at
  0.20–0.26 on every pad from 10 to 80 ppb (step 2a).
- **Dots grow instead of darkening.**  The dot's radius goes from about 8.7
  to 19 px as concentration rises.  So arsenic per pixel cannot grow in a
  straight line with color; most of the information at high concentration
  is in the dot's size and hue.
- **Fit per pad, not per pixel.**  The known concentration belongs to each
  pad, not each pixel.  The simplest fit is
  concentration ≈ a × (sum of yellow) + c × (sum of brown): two numbers from
  18 pads.  A curved fit would need more pads.
- **The two sample sets make different-sized dots at the same
  concentration** (at 20 ppb: radius 15.5 px in set 1, 9.9–10.4 px in
  set 2).  The fit may need to allow for the set.
- **Replicate scatter will probably limit accuracy more than the color
  model.**  The 20 ppb pads spread from +2° to +22° in hue, and earlier
  work found replicate strips differing by about 1.9×.

**6. Summing over the colored area: sound, with a choice to make.**  The dot
study found that the faint fade outside the edge holds 17–20 % of the dot,
but a far-pad offset of 0.04–2 % against the dry pad, summed over the whole
pad, adds a bias.  This is the cutoff-radius choice still open from
`docs/dot_study_2026-09-27.md`.
- **Neighboring pixels are not independent** (added 2026-09-29).  The
  pixel-to-pixel scatter on the pad is mostly the paper's own texture, not
  camera noise (`docs/shading_study_2026-09-29.md`, step 1a).  That texture
  is 1–2 px across: neighbors correlate about 0.2 at 1 px and 0.1 at 2 px.
  So when a pad's pixels are added up, the scatter averages down less than
  it would for independent pixels — roughly as if there were two or three
  times fewer pixels (a rough figure from those correlations).  An error bar
  on a pad total should allow for this.

**7. Filling in cut-off rings: workable, with two conditions.**
- **Use rings around a fitted circle.**  Distances from the half-way edge
  line are wrong where that line runs along the pad edge.  Instead, fit a
  circle to the part of the dot's edge that lies inside the pad, draw rings
  around its center, average each ring over the part that is present, and
  multiply by the full ring's area.
- **This assumes the dot is round.**  C1014's heart-shaped dot shows that is
  not always true.  It also assumes the arsenic that fell outside the pad
  would have landed there if the pad were bigger, which fits the goal (the
  solution's arsenic).
- **The rim needs the same treatment.**  The 4 px rim is left out of every
  pad, so even an uncut dot near the edge loses area there.

### Suggested order, each a step in `pca_study.py`

- **Step 3:** fix the two colors and plot them as spectra (absorbance
  against wavelength, 6 channels).
- **Step 4:** rank channel subsets by precision → choose the channels →
  build the fixed matrix.
- **Step 5:** apply it to every pad: maps of yellow and brown, and of what
  the model leaves unexplained, to check for a third color such as black
  mercury arsenide.
- **Step 6:** add up per pad and compare with concentration, choosing the
  cutoff.
- **Step 7:** fill in the cut-off dots, and compare the totals with and
  without.

## Method caveats

- The map is made from these 18 pads.  A different set of pads gives
  slightly different axes, and so slightly different angles.
- Removing overall darkness also removes the part of each dye that is even
  across the channels, so strengths on the map understate the darkness of
  broad absorbers such as brown.
- Rings are laid out from the royal_blue.B edge for every pad and every
  channel.  The Bayer layers sit up to half a layer pixel apart, which is
  small against 1 px rings.
- Where the dot runs past the pad's 4 px margin (C1021, C1023, C1025), the
  outer rings on that side are missing.
- Only six channels (five wavelengths) are available with the dry-pad
  reference.
