Made by desktop/padanalysis/white_study.show_lambda, 2026-11-11.  Channel: photo_red.R.

THE SET-UP
No dye is involved anywhere in this figure.  One photo of an unstained pad
plays the "post-image" and another photo of the same pad plays the
"pre-image".  Each post-image pixel is divided by a white made from the
pre-image, and the result is turned into absorbance A.  Since both photos
show the same clean paper, the right answer is the same at every pixel (no
stain).  Any pixel-to-pixel scatter in A is error added by the white and by
the camera.

HORIZONTAL AXIS: "blend weight lam on the pixel itself"
How the white for each pixel is made.  It is a weighted average of two
things:
  - the pixel itself: that pixel's value in the pre-image.  It carries the
    paper's own grain (texture) at that spot, which is good, because the
    same grain is in the post-image and divides out.  But it also carries
    the camera's random noise for that one photo.
  - the model white: a smooth version made from the whole pad (a gently
    tilted flat level, times the average darkening of each 1-pixel-wide
    strip along each edge).  It has almost no noise, because it averages
    many pixels, but it knows nothing about the grain at this spot.
lam = 0 uses the model white alone.  lam = 1 uses the pixel alone.
lam = 0.7 means 70 % pixel and 30 % model.  The same lam is used for every
pixel in a panel.  Corner pixels always use the pixel alone.

VERTICAL AXIS: "spread of A"
How much A scatters from pixel to pixel within the panel's group of pixels,
as a standard deviation, in absorbance units.  0.001 A is about 0.23 % of
the light.  Lower is better.  Each line is the typical value over many
photo pairs (root-mean-square).  The spread always includes the
post-image's own camera noise, which no choice of lam can remove, so no
line reaches zero.

THE PANELS
Pixels are grouped by how far they are from the nearest pad edge, in layer
pixels: the interior (8 or more from every edge), 4 to 8, 2 to 4, and the
outermost 0 to 2.  Each panel has its own vertical scale.

THE LINES
  dark gray, solid: photos taken without moving the cartridge (test_image
    IMG0005-0007, the saved C1034 strip).  Same pixels, no lining up.
  colored, solid: pre-images of one sample taken after reseating the strip
    (holders F, G1, H1), lined up onto each other the way the chain lines
    up a post-image.  This adds the effect of lining up.
  dark gray, dotted: what the formula in tex/chain_unified.tex step 5
    predicts for the unmoved photos, from their measured noise and texture.
  dots: the lowest point of each line, i.e. the best lam for that group.

HOW TO READ IT
A line that falls from left to right means the pixel's own grain matters
more than its noise there.  A dip in the middle means a blend beats both
pure choices.  The left end lies far above the right end near the edge,
because the darkening changes too steeply within a 1-pixel strip for the
smooth model to follow.
