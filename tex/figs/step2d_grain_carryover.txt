Made by desktop/padanalysis/white_study.show_carryover, 2026-11-11, from
white_out/carryover.json and white_out/sharpness.json.

THE IDEA
The paper has a fine grain: a fixed pattern of slightly lighter and darker
spots, one or two pixels across, about 0.005-0.007 A in size.  It is found
by subtracting a smoothed copy of a photo from the photo, which removes slow
changes (shading, the dye's broad darkening) and keeps the fine pattern.
The chain divides the post-image by the pre-image so that this grain
cancels.  That works only if the post-image shows the same grain at the
same strength as the pre-image.

HOW TWO PHOTOS ARE "MATCHED"
Take the grain of photo 1 and the grain of photo 2, lined up on the same
paper.  At each pixel multiply the two, and average over the pad's
interior.  Where both are light together or dark together the products are
positive; unrelated patterns average to about zero.  Camera noise is
different in every photo, so it averages away and does not inflate the
match.  Each match is taken at the small extra shift (up to 1 pixel) that
makes it largest, so a lining-up misfit does not look like missing grain.

HORIZONTAL AXIS (all panels): "repeat number"
The order in which the strip's photos were taken: 1 is the first photo of
the series, 5 the fifth.  The post-images of one strip were taken about a
minute apart, starting 2-22 s after reagent 2 ended (samples table).
The order is assumed from the file names (rep2, rep3, ...).

TOP TWO PANELS, VERTICAL AXIS: "c (1 = all of it)"
The share of the paper's grain that shows in that post-image:
    c = (post-image matched with the strip's pre-images)
        / (one pre-image matched with another pre-image of the strip).
The bottom of the fraction is the full-strength match: two photos that both
carry the whole grain.  c = 1 means the post-image shows the grain in full;
c = 0.5 means only half of it.  One panel per channel (LED color and
camera color layer).  The label gives each sample's dye level in that
channel (A = absorbance; bigger means darker).  C1042 and C1044 have only
two repeat files, so their full-strength match rests on one pair of
pre-images and their c is unreliable (values above 1 are not physical).

BOTTOM PANEL, VERTICAL AXIS: "steepest step / pad level"
How sharp the photo is.  At the pad's left and right sides the brightness
drops from the pad to the dark surround.  The largest drop between two
neighboring pixels, as a fraction of the pad's brightness, is bigger in a
sharp photo and smaller in a blurry one.  Solid lines: post-images.
Dashed, lighter lines: the pre-images of the same strip.

HOW TO READ IT
In the C1038-C1041 series (holder F), c falls from near 1 at the first
post-image to about 0.5 by the fifth, with or without dye, and the
post-images' sides get softer over the same repeats: later photos are
blurrier, and a blurrier photo shows less grain.  C1045 (holder G1) does not
get blurrier, yet its c is also below 1, so blur is not the whole story.
